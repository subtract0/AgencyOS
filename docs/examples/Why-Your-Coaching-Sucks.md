## III. Opening Thoughts: "Coaching"

I often say “coaching” whilst cringing on the inside. I still see many good-hearted people who want to help others. They call themselves "coach”. This word, in most countries, is not protected by law. You don't need a degree in coaching to call yourself a coach. You don't even need clients. You don't even have to coach. You can just call yourself "coach.” I did exactly that when I was 20 years old. I even had a business card saying "coach and consultant.” Yet, I had my first client eight years later.

"A Coach Without A Client Isn't A Coach."  
\- *Steve Chandler*

This book is about how to be a **real** coach \- how to serve people powerfully. It is an attempt to teach coaching from the ground up.

Coaching is best learned through coaching and being coached yourself.  
This is not a 50/50 spread. It is 100/100. Both parts are 100% necessary to learn coaching in a similar time frame, as it would take any craftsman to learn his craft.  
Without one or the other, learning is inhibited.  
Like the novice plumber who is only taught how to work but never does any plumbing, or vice versa.  
'Being coached' and 'coaching others' are highly synergistic:

#### **Through 'being coached,’**

you deepen your understanding, raise your level of consciousness, and repeatedly see your own problems dissolve.  
You experience first-hand what it means to be powerfully coached. That is how you develop good taste, which helps your own unique flavour.  
As good writers tend to be avid readers, good coaches tend to have good coaches.

#### **Through 'coaching others,’**

you apply what you learn. You play. You succeed. You fail. You see what works and what doesn't.  
With your coach or supervisor, you can talk it through or even play back a recording of a session.  
Together, you discover what was helpful and explore different paths.  
Every time a client brings a recorded session, and we review it together, it's a game-changer.

#### **Coaching: Art or Craft?**

I was afraid of writing. To me "The Art of Writing" was daunting. This art seemed to be reserved for the most creative and sophisticated humans. If something is an art, it is implied that it is inconceivable, it is based on artistic merit, and it is difficult to get into. On the other hand: Doing a craft is more 'normal.’ It is based on learned skill and mundane technique. Even if it takes years to master it, you can see yourself being able to learn it over time. Good art is often seen as the result of innate talent, while good craft is perceived as the result of learned skill and experience.

Art and Craft are just two different words. Concepts. The way we speak our reality into the world shapes it. So whenever you see a distinction like 'art vs. craft,’ you can choose which concept is more beneficial to you.

To me, writing became much more approachable and easy when I first read the short and brilliant book "On Writing Well" by William Zinsser. It demystified writing for me. I will use his distinction between art and craft to demystify coaching for you. So we will call it a craft, as I am not going to ‘play shaman' to help you with “the mystical and inconceivable art of coaching”. Neither will I chant to your muse to channel your inborn talent. I'll just tell you what is most helpful for me to remember in order to coach well.

For this book to make sense, we look at coaching as **primarily a craft**. This is great news. It means coaching can be learned. Everyone can learn it. To be good at coaching, you **have** to learn it. Nobody is born a good writer, a good plumber, or a good coach. So if you suck at coaching, that's very normal. I still do from time to time. This is why I’m writing this. To remind myself what good coaching looks like, and how to get back on track towards mastery.

We want expertise and competence from the craftsmen we employ. We want our plumber to lay down the gas pipes in our house correctly, so they don't explode. We want the surgeon to know what he is doing when he is cutting tissue inside our body. In the same way, we want our coach to know what he’s doing when we open up, show up fully, and reveal our innermost secret fears and dreams.

Good coaching wakes you up. It raises your level of aliveness and helps you realize things that flew under your radar. This book is trying to do the same. This is why the next part of the book is addressed to **you**, the coach, and blindly accuses you of doing it wrong.

Not because it is true.  
How can it be? I don't know you.

But because you will see what bounces off the wall and what sticks. You will know what sounds true. You might wake up to a fact that you were blind to before. And that will help you improve your work.

So, let's get started and find out why **your** coaching sucks\!

# **1\. Why Your Coaching Sucks**

Here are things, most of which I didn’t want to hear myself. I hope it will help you grow.

From here on, the narrator “**I**” will be the client, referring to the “**you**”, my coach.

**YOUR COACHING SUCKS…**

## … because you didn't learn it.

I hope this book helps with that. Yet even the best book, course, or seminar can't get you there. There is more to it than reading a book. I'm not talking about a certificate or a degree. What matters most is experience: The more you coach and the more you experience powerful coaching, the more you will improve.

… because you didn't invest in your own coach.  
	  
"A coach without a coach is like a doctor who won't see a doctor."  
\- *Steve Chandler*

I remember “not being able to afford a coach” while offering coaching to people, saying a good coach can help them with anything, including money. I hope you see the irony. Get a coach.

… because you're stuck in your own growth, while trying to help others be unstuck

When you think you can coach someone new by repeating old motions, you’re mistaken. Stating that coaching is a craft that can be learned is not to be misunderstood for an assembly-line activity that is to be mindlessly repeated over and over.

To facilitate lasting change, to be truly impactful, you have to be open and present with the client as he is in that moment. You need to work with what he brings to the table, what he is present with. This is why you can’t premeditate your actions or meaningfully prepare for coaching sessions, apart from being mindful with your inner state of being and continuing your own deep inner work with your own coach. 

Coaching is fresh because you bring the freshness. Or better yet: By identifying and removing all the blocks, you help the client tap into his own bubbling freshness. Remember that.

The client is already used to ruminating about what is old and known to him. He doesn’t need help with that. The solution will come only through fresh new thinking.

… because you're secretly unsure if this is the right job for you.

This goes back to the point of: If you’re not okay, it’s hard to help me be okay. If this or another topic is on your mind, you can’t easily be with me in the moment. I would recommend dealing with that to improve your work.

In our session, any thinking about your past or future, any thinking **about you** will be in the way.

Just help me **now**.  
	

… because you're in it for the "quick money.” 

There is none.

… because you act as if the client needs you.  
	  
If you think you **need** something, if you feel lack or scarcity, the client will feel that desperation. If you want to convince me that I **need** you, you act as if I’m not okay.   
	  
	As your client: I’m okay. I just want you to remind me.

… because you're not coaching, but ordering business-cards, posting stuff on Instagram and re-designing your website.  
	  
There is only one way to actually coach.  
It is in a conversation. This can be over the phone, video, in person, in a group, 1-on-1, etc. but it is best when it is done live.	

… because you don't take time to get to know me, giving me superficial discovery calls.  
	  
I can only evaluate what I experience with you. So have me experience your full power right from the start, so I can see whether you can truly help me or not. Don't just talk **about** coaching or my vague goals. This will not show me how you work \- how working with you will benefit me. Instead, look for a good opportunity to jump right in. Leave the conversation on a note where the other can sense a significant impact.

… because you 'treat' me before 'diagnosis’.  
	  
Most people just assume ‘they know’.   
They drop out of the inner spaciousness of ‘not knowing’ and start acting as if they are right in their view.  
	  
You don’t have to know the ‘right’ answer to the question you’re asking. Some of the best questions are open in nature. Such that an exploration feels new and fresh for everyone involved.

People don’t like being rhetorically ‘led’ down a predictable and narrow track. I still do that from time to time, and it never works. In the end the best outcome of this is just an intellectual understanding that matches my model. Not a genuine transformation.

**Good coaching comes from not-knowing**, asking honest questions, listening. Not rhetorical objects to trick me. Not to ‘make me see what you already know’. Real, curious questions, to understand and to see something new.

Not **your** ‘new’, **my own** new.

… because the conversations often feel overwhelming instead of helpful.

One insight is not only "enough.”  
One is the best number of insights.

It is a sign of mastery to know when to end a conversation.

If you want, create an agreement with me in advance. Explain that the amount of time we use for the session is irrelevant. Instead, we aim for **one** good insight. Ask me if that is what I want.  
You can even let me decide when I'm done for the session. In case you recognize me always seeking out more coaching and more insights, you can remind me of the above. If you feel the need, offer a follow-up call later, instead of continuing after that **one** good insight.

When I get it, I want you to be my witness. Call me out. Say “I see you.” Let me sit with it for as long as it takes. Then ask me about what I realise. What I just heard. Be curious. Let me talk until I'm truly done. **Witness**. Then ask what that means for me. Then ask me what is now possible in light of this insight. Then ask me what my first step to put that into action will be.

Instead of wanting me to say "that was a great session, thank you\!", ask me what has changed in my world. If it is a powerful insight, that's all I can work with \- for now.

This is why I want you to stop searching for more, after one insight.

## … because you’re responding to your perception of me, not to who I truly am.

… because you’re in a conversation with your mind—not with me.

Metaphor: If I am lost in the woods, it is not enough to stand outside of the forest and shout “come out of there, it’s beautiful out here\!”

I want you to come find me and take my hand and offer to lead me.

Understand. Meet me where I am. Be there with me. Then, and only then, lead me out of there into that beauty that you know.

"People don't care what you know   
until they know that you care."  
\- *Dr. Bill Pettit*

… because you sometimes think of what to say next, instead of creating space for me.  
	  
Aim at landing in silence. Truly hearing **all of it**. Taking the time to **not** speak, for as long as you don't know what to say.  
You always choose whether you assume **or** listen. You can only choose one.

Powerful coaching starts from silence. When responding, pause and really let it sink into silence. Listen to what comes out of silence. It is essential in a deep coaching conversation to always keep some attention on the silence underneath. (see chapter 5\)

… because you believe my problems to be true.  
	  
They really  aren't. I just think they are.  
Understand me. Be a good friend. Care. Be there for me.  
Don't try talking me out of "not being okay.” It won't work.  
But always, always keep in sight the true nature of 'my problem.’

… because you don’t believe my feelings to be true.  
	They are, I feel them. Even if they don't lead me on the right path, my feelings are there.

If you brush them off too quickly, if you try to correct me before you’ve connected with me, I’ll feel like you're not hearing me — like you're trying to skip a step. Maybe I’m wrong about what caused my feelings, but the feelings themselves? They’re alive in me right now. And if you meet me there — even briefly — I’ll trust you to help me look deeper.

… because you believe my reasons for my feelings.   
	  
When I feel bad, I usually project it onto something seemingly external. I don't want  to keep it, so I give it away, right?  
Then my anger is “the dog's fault” since he shit on my floor.  
But giving it makes me keep it.

“To have, give all to all.”  
*\- A Course in Miracles*

I was naively and innocently doing it myself. Blaming the dog for **what is**. Creating guilt. When I see this, when I understand the perfection of **what is** and let go of judgement, I stop suffering.

So instead of believing that **“my dog is causing me to feel bad”**, and helping me cope with my bad feelings, I want my coach to help me see my innocent mistake in perception. 

As a coach you know and expect me to falsely think it is the outside world that is causing my feelings. Your job is to help me realise the outside world only reflects what I hold in mind.

… because you’re comfortable with just saying “it's okay that (client) feels sick, poor, fat, and depressed from time to time.”  
	  
It's always “okay” to feel sick, poor, fat, and depressed whenever I feel it. But I don't pay you to paint my glass half-full instead of half-empty. I want you to help me realise it's a 'painting' in the first place.

I want you to help me change my world.   
Not just thinking more positively about my current life situation.  
Understanding the difference between **my life situation** (thinking) and **life** (being).

## … because you're not saying what you're afraid of saying.

… because you’re pleasing, **not** serving.

Only the ego can be pleased or displeased.  
It is very tempting to do so. The client is doing it to himself as well. He is innocently and unconsciously pleasing the ego: fearing its hallucinations of the future, honoring its wants and needs, listening to its laments, following its orders to “change things for the better in the future”.

… because you want me to like you.  
…which is **not** even the real you.

If you’re withholding your truth, you’re hiding. What do you think I will do?   
I will be afraid of saying what I think. I will be hiding. Our masks, that we hold up because of fear, will continue the conversation.

Not much will happen. 

Most people experience this in their everyday conversations. They don’t need to pay a coach for that. 

You need to be willing to be disruptive if you want your coaching to be actually life-changing.   
This does not always seem easy.   
And for it to be helpful, it needs love and empathy. The truth is often what changes lives. Your job is to challenge the ‘reality’ of the fixed views that are holding your client back.

Tell me what your ego is afraid of saying. Please. That’s the whole point.

… because you make it more than 0% about you.

It's not about you. Not at all.

Look, it’s really not about **you**.  
Whatever we’re doing should be valuable for **me**. Not new and original, not clever, not entertaining, not persuasive and not important **to you**.

**Helpful for me.**

That is the main thing to optimize for.  
Do what comes through you.  
Remember: You’re serving and not pleasing .  
Your purpose is to be helpful.  
Silence and stillness is a strong stance.  
If you don’t know what to say, say nothing at all.

… because you want to come across as a strong and successful role model.  
	  
It’s not about you, though.  
Just be real instead.

Be human, be vulnerable.  
Be whatever you are right now.  
People don’t pay you to be a role-model.  
They pay you to help them.

… because you pretend.

You pretend you know when you don't.  
You pretend you don't know when you do.  
Be real with me. Be human.   
It's not about you, anyway.

… because you take yourself seriously.

"The key to failure is taking yourself seriously. The key to success is taking your work seriously."  
\- *Steve Chandler*

I like the word 'sincerely' even better than 'seriously’. The less of ‘you’ there is in a conversation, the better.

## … because you try to give me something that you don't have yourself. 

… because you talk of what you've heard, not what you **know**.  
	  
Nobody will buy it if you don't see it and live it yourself. Your words can’t convey the truth. They can just point at something true. Just repeating the words of wise people is like taking useful street signs and carrying them to my place to help me find that place you found.  
A+ for effort. These pointers might point to something, but they just confuse me.  
Stay with what you know. Keep it easy and simple. If you don't know anything worth pointing to, be more selfish and get it for yourself first.

… because you're preaching your gospel instead of solving my problem.  
	  
It can be neuro-linguistic programming, transcendental meditation, contemplation, or your spiritual understanding.   
I don't care. I told you about my problem. I don't want you to say "Have you heard about the ‘three principles of mind, consciousness and thought’ as uncovered by Sydney Banks?"  
I don't care about your stuff. It's about me here. Help me. I want a coach, not a missionary.

The coaching ego: "Whoa, are you saying my method, model, or understanding isn't valid?"

No. It can be awesome. But I hired you to solve my problem, not to preach something that I can watch on YouTube.

Help with my specific problem. If I'm better off and you think I want to know more, I'll probably ask you. If you think I need help, don't just go into lecture mode, just ask if there is something else I want to explore deeper. Be brief and err on the side of telling less. Let me ask or google for the missing pieces instead of overloading me with information that I didn't ask for.

If you want a deep impact for your clients and use the greatest leverage, **be present**. Be open to what comes up at the moment. That way, whatever you say is relevant to the client's situation.

Often, the right metaphors might jump at you, and those are much more useful than some theoretical construct from a book. Metaphors or stories let people **experience** and understand an alternative view first-hand from within. Theory and models on the other hand, are usually an ineffective cognitive exercise. 

Understanding (intellectually) that a joke is supposed to be funny is not the same as breaking out into uncontrollable laughter.

The ego will always love to understand everything intellectually, but it is not vital for you to experience a radical and lasting change from within.

… because you want to convince me or you think that you're right, and I'm wrong.  
	  
After all, I’m coming to you for the solution, right?  
Wrong.  
Coaching is not consulting.  
I come to **myself** for the solution, and I pay you to remind me of that and lead me to a place where I can see it for myself.

Never convince me. I will just get defensive.  
Understand me. See how my view on life makes sense for me. See how I am "right,” as seen from where I stand first.  
Truly understand.   
Then, and only then, ask me if I want to try on an alternative view for fun.  
Respect my answer. Coaching is inherently an eye-level conversation.

… because you talk too much.

… because you think you're paid to say smart stuff.

If you don’t know what to say, say nothing. This is the best way to serve.  
If you’re still unsure about that, talk to me about this in advance and find out for yourself.

… because you are not grounded.

In a way, coaching isn’t what you “do”.  
	You’re **not** paid for what you are doing.  
	You’re paid for what **you are**.

Don’t try to lead where you haven’t been.   
Even if you heard about the existence of the ‘location.’   
Even if you conceptually believe it truly exists.  
If I get lost, you might not be able to help me see through the mist. How can you? You don’t even know where I’m going or how to know when I’m ‘there.’

You can only lead someone deeper than you were by accident (or by giving him psychedelics).

So, do your own deep inner work first. Get a coach. Be selfish. Get your shit together.

You can't give away what you don't have.

"If I have 20 dollars in my pocket,   
I can not give you 50."   
\- *Sydney Banks*

Say what you live. Live what you say. This is about being authentic.

If you’re not, I can smell it from afar.

… because you facilitate an exchange of **information** rather than a process of **transformation**.  
	  
My transformation happens **not** in the time we talk, but in the **instant** I understand.  
You want to give Information?   
I want to change my life.

You want to explain because you were taught in school to do that to demonstrate that you “understand”.

**But that’s not how I** **understand**.  
Explain less, ask more.  
   
**Understand me truly.**   
That is 90% of the job.

Not making **me** understand, not getting **me** to see it, not preaching **your** point.  
Just understand me fully. With love and acceptance.  
When you do, I will be curious, open, and will often just get it on my own.

If you aim for transformation, you’ll stop ‘coaching’ over e-mail or text messages, and stop trying to make me ‘learn something useful **from** you’.

If you see it as a transaction \- money for information \- you’re on the wrong track.  
Instead, be a guide, my witness, and my companion on my path.

If you saw something new that you think would be helpful to talk about, great.   
You want me to watch a video or read an article because you know I will benefit greatly? Good.  
   
Please don’t just send me the link with ‘FYI’ in the title. 

Instead, tell me about the context in which the material is valuable **for me.** 

Let me know you as having a track-record of sending me **highly relevant** material. By recognizing what is relevant for me specifically, you demonstrate you understand me, and I see your value as my trusted advisor.

… because you engage in philosophy instead of actual coaching.

Don’t coach the hypothetical.  
Instead, ask about actual living concrete examples. 

	If someone comes to me and wants to handle a problem, I will never work with a general problem description.

Imagine a client saying: “Alex, I’m *always* inhibited by my limiting beliefs. I procrastinate and don’t know what to do about it. Help me.”

I will not get to work here, because nothing real is expressed. It is just general statements of limitation and theories, but no real experience to talk about. Thus, I will ask: “Help me understand you better. Tell me an actual example and how that played out the last time it happened. What happened exactly?”

It’s normal for the client to have resistance here. Hiding the actual thing is part of the ego's “protection” strategy.

I will continue asking for an example, until the client shares something like “Okay, so last Saturday I wanted to do my homework at home, but when I started I just felt bad and watched YouTube instead. Then I felt even worse and kept watching until I fell asleep at 2AM.”

And even then, I will dig deeper:   
“Can you help me understand your inner landscape during that time? What was the underlying thinking/feeling? What were the ideas that were haunting you, that were really hard to take?”

“What did YouTube do for you? What if it was not the problem but a solution?” I don’t think the client is weak or dumb, we just don't fully understand what's up yet\!

“What did YouTube seem to solve (temporarily)?” “Let's zoom in to “when I started…”, you were there, let's slow down and examine what actually happened there, in detail.”

As you can see here, by getting into an **actual example** we get much more traction and can look at how the problem actually appears, not just what the client thinks **about** the problem.

## … because you don't lead.

	  
There is no 'talking too little.’   
There is only 'not leading.’

I pay you, so you lead me to where I want to be. If you're merely adding to what I'm saying, there is a dimension of coaching yet to be uncovered. Hire a powerful coach and experience it firsthand, so you know what is possible.

Leading is not dictating. Leading can come in the form of beautiful questions opening up my potential. Leading can come as seeing a clear vision and sharing what you see.

And if you don’t know what to say or where to go? Say nothing.   
If you just hold the space for something to show up, it doesn’t have to show up for you. Often the client will magically find their own new point of view, just within the extra space you opened up.  
… because you work without clear intentions.  
	  
It is helpful to uphold the intention to see something new in every conversation. You, not the client. He will follow.  
	

… because you work without permission.  
	  
Even in a healthy coaching-relationship on eye level without imposed hierarchy, at some point the coach has to disrupt the client’s thinking to shake their world, or to even put it upside-down.

The first time you try this as a novice coach, you run the risk of coming across as a dick.

As a client I will feel as if it’s “You against me”. As if you’re attacking what I hold as true.

Besides being ineffective, this will cause resistance. This is why many coaches don’t like going there at first. Yet, you want to go there.

Growth will be found at the edge of the known and comfortable. But at the same time, you can’t just push someone off a cliff. I believe you never need to use force to achieve anything worthwhile. You can keep the integrity of the eye level relationship and stay on the same side of the table as your client's companion. You do this by continuously being respectful and keeping a sense of consent. So make sure you ask for permission before you are doing stunts near cliffs. (TK: reference to the evolving coaching relationship chapter below)

A powerful coaching relationship gets co-created over time with integrity and curiosity. Therefore, some questions, that work as an opener for some potential:  
“What you just said about X. Would you…  
… be open to play with that?”  
… try on a different view for fun?”  
… allow me to challenge that?”  
… be open to put that aside for now?”  
	… to consider a different view?”  
… be ready for …?”

As your client I **want** to grow beyond my limited zone of comfort. Ideally, you can give me the bold direction (off the cliff) not with a push from behind, but by going right by my side, be right there with me.

With your steadfast knowing that nothing real can be threatened there, I will develop the sense that I am safe to explore with you, no matter where we end up going.

… because you don't fully believe in your client 

As a client, I want you to be my secret greatest fan or refuse to work with me.  
I want to pay you, and pay you well. But I don’t want you to just do it for the money. I want to pay for **my commitment** to my development and my growth. And still it is invaluable if it is **my growth**, that you do it all for.

I want to feel held by my coach, even if we are not on the phone right now. I want you to be there with me in spirit, and recognize the true me at my best and at my worst. I want to think your support has no limits, and that all that is limiting me is “me”.  
	  
… because you think you will change your client.

You can't. It is impossible.

Even if I say “Please change me”, I must have judged I’m wrong in some way. If you try to change me, you reinforce the false belief that there is something wrong with me. The natural response will be resistance or defense. And no matter how much new-year-resolutions-style-commitment and accountability we throw at it, the inner dividedness will hurt.

If lasting change is called for, change will occur as soon as it is ripe. Then, change is effortless and flows naturally.

The medium by which change is facilitated is not outside force, but insight from within.

… because you don’t build rapport during the process 	  
Remember: A coaching session gets cocreated by your clients and your own mind. If you don’t ask for feedback frequently, you’re being ignorant for the vast potential, your client can offer to the process of his inner growth. 

You can use these questions that get you on track after talking:

"Tell me how you're hearing this?"   
"Does this seem helpful to you?"   
"Am I missing the point of your question?"   
"Does this look doable or reasonable?"  
"Are we going too fast?"

#### **ART/CRAFT vs. SERVICE**

Many people confuse art/craft and service. Some are full of entitlement, speaking about how unfair it is that they do their art/craft but don't get paid — musicians, artists, content creators, writers, producers, woodworkers, etc. I heard them all, including myself.

It is easy to understand why they don't get paid:  They don't **serve**.

If I'm painting all day long, and even if I like everything I make, but nobody wants to buy my work, I might be a great artist, but not of service to anyone.

But if I do commissions work, painting what is needed, on-demand, I will often even get paid in advance.

The same goes for coaching. If you just talk to people, if you converse fluently and beautifully, if they are as inspired as you are, if all of this is fun and uplifting, that's great\! Consider yourself great at conversing. Maybe even at “trying to help”. 

That’s all fine as long as you don’t expect someone to pay you. If you want to be paid, you got to serve. Find out what the person wants. What is needed? Get very clear on that. You need this clarity to know if it’s a fit to work together. And for them, clarity on the situation alone is a great service in and of itself.  
Then, when you have clarity and altitude, show how you can support them and what it would cost them. If they want to have your support, create a strong agreement with the client. 

A strong agreement casts out any expectations. All open questions about “what is going to happen?”, everything unclear is at least talked about and acknowledged. Most of the uncertainty can be taken out of the equation if it is clear to both parties what is part of the service and what is not.

Example: “In our coaching together, we will commit to working weekly for up to 120min on the phone, in which we will … “ (for full Agreement-blueprint, see attachment)

This will serve as a strong frame for your work.  
I create individual agreements with every client, where everything but price is negotiable before we agree to it.  
Tell your client they are free to add or subtract a point in your agreement and that you want them and yourself to be 100% on board with everything before you start.

# **2\. What Coaching is not**

Before we talk about what coaching **is** (next chapter), let us first clean the slate. By looking at common misconceptions you will learn what coaching **is not** and open your mind for what coaching is. 

Coaching is **not** about you.

You wouldn't believe me if I added:  
"nothing is about you."   
So we'll just start with   
"coaching is not about you.”

Coaching has nothing to do with you.  
In a coaching session, the spotlight is on the client, not on you.   
Whatever your ego thinks, doesn’t matter.  
Free yourself of "you" before every session.   
It's not about that. You will be of great service, by this alone. Demonstrating this, will give your client a lot of space to be heard and understood. 

Over time the absence of your “me” will inspire them to free themselves of that concept as well.

Coaching is **not** something you do **to**, **at**, or **for** me, but **with** me.

The best coaching happens when there is no separation. In such sessions, you **don't**  
explain **to** me,  
lecture **at** me,  
“lay it out” or “have good advice” **for** me.

You can be sure, that I’ll be defensive:   
		I’m going to say “Yes, but...”  
I’m going to object.   
I’m going to tell you why it works for you, but not for me.

Instead, just...  
have a conversation **with** me,  
share **with** me,  
explore **with** me,  
**be with me.**

Together we unfold what holds us back.   
I become aware. It disintegrates.

With you at my side, I find the insight that I seek.

Rather than listening **to** me, ‘**just listen**’ \- and invite me to do the same.

‘**Just listening**’ is powerful.

There is much more than "me" to be found.

Coaching is **not** about "you".

And if you're in the miracle-business, you'll help me to look beyond "me" as well.

Coaching is **not** shaming, blaming or pointing fingers at me.

Coaching is **not** building up my pride, putting me on a pedestal.

Both of these will just make me identify even more with my self-image.  
I didn't pay you to paint a nicer picture over the ugly picture in my head.  
I unknowingly paid you to help me understand that it's just a picture in the first place.

\- Not my true self.

"I" can't be my 'True Self', if I'm observing it. Get it for yourself, before you try to give it away.

Coaching is eye level conversation.   
Just be real with me.   
Be human.   
Understand me.  
Help me understand.

Lead. 

If leading and eye level sounds paradoxical to you, sit with that. Once resolved, you'll lead with more power and less force.

Coaching is **not** forceful.

Be powerful, not forceful.  
Don't ever pressure me into action. If you're doing accountability work, propose a game to me. Let me know that all of what is to be agreed upon is up for debate before we agree to it. Make sure both of us are 100% on the same page.

Then, and only then, agree. Make sure to create windows for revision and re- negotiation of the agreement.

Working with power, NOT force, is working with a thick buffer of permission. Really make that space so safe, that even if one of you tried, they couldn't hurt themselves, like in a padded cell in a psychiatric hospital.  
Inside that space, cut their shit to shreds with your sharpest katana.

If you don't work with permission, your client, who opened up to you, will close up again and be defensive. Of course he is, he didn't know you would bring the sword. 

But if you, at the doorstep, show them the sword, let them inspect it, find out that all it can cut is thought, not flesh or bone. They'll give it back to you, and they'll invite you inside and explicitly ask you to use it on all the bullshit you find.

THEN, and only THEN, go all "Kill Bill" on their nonsense. Instead of closing up and defending, they will open up even more and let the exorcism happen.

Haha, what a metaphorical rollercoaster...

Coaching is **not** something you do alone.

Coaching is a two-player game.

Here, coaching is actually different from crafts like writing, plumbing or coding.

Coaching is **not** a haircut.

Sure, you're leading our conversation  
\- showing up fully, bringing your full presence power to the conversation \-  
but if I keep my mouth shut, there will be no coaching.

So please: Lead me into co-creating this experience for my own benefit. Help me to own my part of our coaching so it serves me best.

You can't MAKE me talk.   
You can only LET me talk.   
Facilitate. Intrigue me. Be curious.

It's deceptively simple:  
When you're loud, people lean away.  
When you're quiet, people lean in.

Coaching is **not** psychoanalysis or therapy.  
(Past-Oriented)

It is not talking about the past for hours and making 'sense' of what happened.  
It is not about learning more about the client’s 'identity'.

It's about what IS right now.  
It is about understanding the very nature of concepts like 'sense' or ‘identity'.

"Do not try and bend the spoon, that's impossible.  
Instead, only try to realize the truth...  
**There is no spoon.**  
Then, you'll see that it is not the spoon that bends, it is only yourself."  
\- *Spoon Boy from ‘The Matrix’*

Coaching is **not** consulting   
(Competence/Information).

As a coach, you know that continuing to analyse the complicated questions in my head will keep me at the same level.  
In the best case, I'll exercise my analytical muscles and gain more data about that content level.  
As a client, I might become more proficient at that level, but I won't even consider that another level even exists.

Please at least help me understand what game I'm playing before you go tactical and answer my questions. That is, before you play with me on my content level.

As a client, on many occasions I prevented my coaches from working properly by bullying them into answering my all-so-important questions about my business, while they were trying to point me to something deeper and help me to operate from a higher level of consciousness.

As my coach, hold your ground and gently continue pointing with power and resolve.

Know that there is more to it than I see.   
Even more than you see yourself.

Hold that open space in patience, so we can discover together.

Coaching is **not** Hypnosis   
(Subliminal/Unconscious).

Don't try to sneak something in behind my back, or I might leave my guards up.  
Don't hide anything, or I might hide from you.

Coaching is **not** a subliminal affair.

Good coaching is the opposite.   
It is simple and it brings things to light.   
It is not necessary to play a magician, manipulating the attention of the crowd to hide the initial trick.   
It does not attempt to craft the perfect “light-pill” and sneak it into our “real problems”. they are not real. They will dissolve in light. (TK, später verbessern)

Here, infinite potential for inner wisdom, love and growth lies. 

Help me to remember the presence I am. Help me see my unconscious thinking and hidden assumptions. It is useful to know what you are not, to find what is your essence.

“What you are unaware of controls you.  
What you are aware of you can control.”  
*\- Anthony De Mello, ‘Awareness’ (Book)*

Coaching is **not** "Good Advice".

Don't show me a 7-Step-List of things to consider, that you put together for your target audience.  
Don't begin sentences with   
"If I were you, I'd just ..."

Remind me that I am the most powerful person in my life and there already is everything I need to solve my “real” problems inside me right now.  
Help me to see it, live it, and embody it.  
Help me to step into that power and own it.

When I do, I won't need your "tips".   
I'll know what to do.  
Giving out tips comes from the coach’s confusion between information and transformation.

The transaction is **not**:  
I pay you, you give me useful information.

What I want is:  
You help me to reconnect with my own wisdom, step into my true power and own it. When I do, I always know what to do.

Coaching is **not** "perfect" \- **not** even "right".

You and your best friend usually don't have to think about what is the right thing to say to each other. You converse. You're with each other. You play and have fun.

For some reason we forget that coaching can be that playful and effortless.

You **can** work without thinking.   
You can work without being one step ahead. Without playing this mental chess game to out-maneuver my belief  system.   
In fact, dropping all that will bring out your best work.

Just be real and point to the truth you see.  
There is no "right" way to coach. So take a deep breath and exhale all of what you think you "have to be doing."  
And I know very well that this contradicts the very existence of this book.  
I don't care.

Coaching is **not** engaging with the little bitch that I might think I am at that moment.

Coaching is talking with my true Self \- the aware consciousness, which is currently identified with that ‘bitchy’ thinking. Lost and confused.

You know what I **truly** am, aside from my thinking in this moment. Talk to **that**\!

I learned this from my wife Olga. When I'm angry towards her, she is just loving instead of being defensive.  
In her head she must be like  
"Awww, he's confused. He obviously would never mean that. I know he loves me when he's not confused. I love him too."

My anger just poofs into nothingness when it is meeting defenseless silence. It doesn't need any 'pointing out' on someone's part. I notice at some point.

Coaching is **not** over when the call ends.

Most of your clients won't realize, they invest in a significant portion of your mental capacity as a coach.  
If you have a practice with five clients, each of us gets about 20% of your professional capacity.

Don't get me wrong: I didn't ask you to worry about me. Never worry about your clients. You'll just put more weight on their shoulders.

Rest in knowing that I’m fine.  
   
**“My well-being is never at stake,**   
**only my well-thinking.”-AM**

Instead, spend time before and after each session and just be open to see new ways of supporting me.

And most importantly: Be my herald who holds my banner. Be my fan and a true believer.

"But Alex, I can't just fake this."

If you have to fake this, please firmly say "No" to working with me.

I don’t want to work with a coach who just does it for the money.

In my early days as a novice coach, I refused to work with the very first person who wanted to pay me thousands of euros, because I didn't fully believe in what they wanted to do. This was one of my hardest and best decisions.

\[Coach to his Client:\]  
"Don't believe in yourself.   
Believe in me, \[...\] who believes in you."   
\- *Tengen Toppa Gurren Lagann, Episode 1 (Anime)*

That quote is an inside joke to satisfy my inner nerd, but it's so true:  
Whenever it was hard for me to believe in myself, it was much easier to believe in the iron resolve of my powerful coach, who always believed in me 100%.

Be **that** coach.

# **3\. What Coaching is**

After we understood what coaching is not, we now look at what coaching is. Becoming a great coach necessitates opening your mind. Nevertheless, I want you to remember one thing:

Before you haven’t **experienced** what good coaching is, there is no book, no audio, that can reach the level of depth a real session can give you. 

I want you to take part in my experience of the first powerful coaching session I had with Rikky. 

We met in her webinar called "How to get coaching clients without marketing." Rikky is a powerful coach, and entrepreneur, who was living the life I thought I wanted.

I must have come across like a chauvinist on multiple occasions, but the truth is: There was no human on this planet I respected more than Rikky.  
Rikky saw me.  
She always looked straight into my innermost core and listened for my greatest potential.  
Much of what I know about coaching I learned from her.  
Her coaching felt very low-key.  
Most of the time I was talking.  
I felt like she was listening very deeply.  
90% of what she said seemed to be "yes".  
Towards the end of the session she would lead with immense power and ask questions pointing me in the right direction.  
After each call I was feeling so alive, so powerful, so good and hopeful.  
I loved every session with Rikky.  
The beginning was rough, though.  
I wanted her to be my business coach and help me build the six-figure coaching business she had.  
I think she wanted to help me find the spiritual nature of the mind.  
I was constantly talking and asking technical questions about business and coaching, which took up all the room in our calls.  
At some point she stepped in and proposed an alternative: For one session, we talk about whatever is on my business mind and needs solving. In the other, we go deep and I let her lead.  
I was very intrigued but sceptical.  
I didn't really believe in "all that spirituality  nonsense" of these "woo-woo people".  
Here my words are failing me.  
It definitely was a ride, and I was often resisting, yet it helped me immensely.

#### **Helpful Metaphor:**

Always look a centimeter above your client's current eye level (their level of consciousness). Don’t believe their littleness. Connect to where their eyes actually would be if they held their head high.  
Give them that space to grow into.  
Then, when they inevitably stand taller at some point, remember to readjust and give them space to grow more.  
At the end of your session, you'll have expanded the horizon of their mind like a rocket lighting the sky in a dark night. 

Let me now paint the broad picture of what coaching is, before going into more detail in the following chapters.

Coaching is a **game** you play with your client.  
Coaching is the best form of non-solo mental and emotional **hygiene**.  
Coaching helps someone **move**.  
Coaching is showing up fully. **Being you.**

Truly being there, present in the moment.  
Vulnerable. Powerful. Real.  
Caring. Understanding. Supporting. Loving.

# **4\. What happens in a Coaching Session**

I can see why many colleagues don't like to write about this.  
Without context and a real human connection, there is no life in it.  
This chapter serves as a broad plan that can help you help others.  
It is useful to understand the sequence:

#### **1\. Bonding: DEEPLY LISTEN TO UNDERSTAND**

Let go of everything you believe, think, remember or project of the world and your client.  
Let go of everything.  
Only when you're empty, something new can come in.  
This state of deep listening is the state that I and everyone of your clients is secretly looking for \- even if they say they want a million dollars. Remember these words.

Be with me. Truly recognize me.  
Don't just believe that there must be potential there, somewhere.  
Talk from what you KNOW.  
Tell me what you're seeing right now, at this moment.

Go deeper in finding out what makes me tick. Ask questions.  
Focus on seeing the human being, understanding my world.

Question my thinking.  
Not to mess me up, but to understand me fully.  
Be a detective for my hidden and unquestioned assumptions, rules and systems.

**Assumptions:**   
"X is Y" (Non-verbal, unquestioned). Example: "The World is flat" in the 7th century BC.

**Rules:**   
"If X, then Y"

**Systems:**   
"X is because of Y"

**Bullshit:**   
"EIGENTLICH wollte ich ..."

**Victim language:**   
"I should have (not)."  
"It should (not) be X."  
"I may (not) X."  
"I must (not) X."  
"I can't X."

**Good reasons example:**   
"I can't forgive my rapist."

#### **2\. OPEN UP FOR POTENTIAL**

Lead powerfully. Guiding me, being fearless, failing.  
What are you most afraid to say? Get my permission and SAY THAT.  
Because I don't pay you to please me but to serve me.  
I don't want you to pat my back and say the exact same thing all my friends were saying.  
If you only repeat what I want to hear, there will be no change, no movement.  
But I will be blind to it. I still might tell you   
"That was a great session, thanks Coach\!" because it was so pleasing to the ego.

Don't be happy with that.

Peter Drucker is reported to say to his consulting clients: “Don’t tell me you had a wonderful meeting with me. Tell me what you’re going to do on Monday that’s different?”

If the world of your client didn't shift, nothing was accomplished. You're not there to chat, but to coach.

**Looking for potential.**

"To hell with circumstances,   
I create opportunities."   
\- *Bruce Lee*

One of your main benefits to me is your third-person view. You see more than me, not because you're smarter, but because I'm in the middle of it and you're detached and outside. 

The paradox is that when I'm confused, I'm often too confused to notice. 

When I feel stuck, hopeless or don't know what to do, help me find an opportunity, potential, and the open path.

You don't have to see it, just make us both look in the right direction. The direction of potential. Know that there is potential and keep pointing at the open space. 

In my experience, doing that will often make me find it faster than you. Consider holding back your solution for a second. Before you give "advice", make sure I have opened up and have seen new opportunities for myself. Only if I am open enough to see my own, I can be open enough to hear yours.

Bruce Lee didn't say   
"I find opportunities"  
but   
"I **create** opportunities".

"Creating" is **not** "finding".

It is not a search-operation.  
It is a limitless creative manufacturing operation.

You will never have exhausted the possibility space. Never. It is infinite.

A lesser known quote from Bruce Lee makes that more clear:

"I’ve always been buffeted by circumstances because I thought of myself as a human being affected by outside conditioning. Now, I realize that **I am the power** that commands the feeling of my mind and from which circumstances grow."

- *Bruce Lee*

Thomas Edison famously puts it in:

"When you have exhausted all possibilities, remember this: You haven't."

#### **3\. FACILITATE INSIGHT FROM WITHIN**

Help me raise my level of consciousness \- to put what I am going through into perspective.

Explore with me the nature of the human mind.

Hold the space where miracles happen.

Silence.

Give me the gift of silence.

Silence is not "nothing". It is a space you can deliberately create and uphold. With all your power.  
It's like a vacuum. To create a vacuum, you spend a lot of energy to get the molecules out of the container.

As a coach, I seemed naturally bad at this at first. I love to talk and in many of my early coaching sessions I was talking way more than 50% of the time. So, this is not something that comes easy to me.

Understanding the power of silence is critical to becoming a better coach.  
In the right context, the silence will pull on the mind of the client and will draw out everything that wants to come up.  
And when it does, the client will tell you.  
Often they will crack up laughing.   
This is a good sign that the coaching is working.  
You can delight in their laughter and join.

Remember:   
It's not about what you throw in there.  
It's not about your lukewarm insights you've read in a book two days ago.  
It's not about you. It's about them. Now.

When **you** tell **them** your "secret sauce", they might find it interesting, but it will feel external.  
They might resist, they might forget, they might object and say:  
"Yeah, sure that works for you, because you are X. But I'm different."

Ever heard that before after giving the perfect piece of advice?

I did and I naively blamed the client or myself:  
"Why won't they listen?”   
“They said they want to hear it\!"  
“Stupid me. It was my fault.”

Remember: You're a coach here. Not a lecturer.  
They already have twelve pieces of advice on that topic. They already tried to make sense of that mess. Another piece might not be of much help.

Go to another dimension.   
Not Information. Transformation.  
Show them a different place. With the perspective where they can drop the twelve pieces of well-intentioned advice and don't even need it anymore. When **they** see something new that is true, for themselves, they will keep it forever. They can't resist, they can't forget, they can't help it but "just know."  
That is what you are aiming for.

#### **4\. Figuring it out vs. Creating from Nothing** 

“We can not solve our problems with the same level of thinking that created them.” \- *Albert Einstein*

This is what I call 'go to another dimension'.  
One of my mentors really listens to her clients and understands them. When she is sure she got the full picture, she explains it back and makes sure she got it right, and when the client feels fully understood, she just says something like:

"Okay, I think I understand now. Thanks for sharing all of that with me. I honor your trust. If you like, we can put that aside for now and look at something different. Don't worry, we can come back to that later. But first let’s talk about something else. Is that cool?"

Most people rejoice at the opportunity to 'put that problem aside'. They know deep down that chewing on it further won't solve it.

Sydney Banks writes in his immensely powerful book "The Missing Link":   
"If your thoughts wander onto a negative and rocky path, don’t take them too seriously. Refrain from analyzing, because, I guarantee you, you will analyze yourself forever, never reaching an end, and fail bitterly to find peace of mind."

Now that they are ready to put that aside, you're well on track to shift gears, to go to another dimension, to talk about what you see, what you know. Right this moment. Tell your story and what helped you. Not lecture, share. Stay real, keep the intimate connection. And always check back if this is helpful.

In the vast majority of cases you will find the client light up and relax. When the mind becomes open and more present, these old artifacts of the past won't bother them anymore \- without any "solution".

Again, and maybe this is redundant (TK):

I create a Forgiveness-Preframe, by telling people what our purpose is, and it goes something like this:

We are talking on eye-level here. I’m not any better or wiser than you. Coaching is a game in which we make our rules. The only one I want to propose for now is the basic premise: My job is to help you see something new.   
This does **not** mean “to tell you something I already know”.   
It means “to help you let go of your inner resistance and fears and guilt and shame around this topic, because when we are free and open, new insight will show up as fresh ideas.

Fact is: if you come to me, that seeming problem is not 45 min old. Many people come to me with a decade worth of suffering. With tightly packed baggage of reasons why they are broken and lacking, with ideas how to cope, and reasons why healing is not possible. 

I guarantee you, they are always mistaken.

How can I know? Well, fact is: They come to me with it. They are not done with it. Whatever they think is the problem, whatever they think is the solution, etc. \- if it was true and the full picture, why would they need me?

In reality they come to me because this is not the full picture, and they are confused about what the problem is, and they can’t see a solution at all.

Example:   
“Alex, I often procrastinate and end up watching TikTok instead of working. That’s probably because of my relationship with my dad. When I was little he would beat me when I didn't clean my room, and now I feel wrong and ashamed whenever I am in my room, if it’s untidy. But I also feel immobilized and can’t tidy it up. Help me\!”

After being with and fully understanding the client, it comes down to something like this:

Well we have two options:

1\. I can believe all the ideas and stories that are presented here. Then I would have to say: The cause of your suffering is in the past. We can’t change that. So… unfortunately… You’re f\*cked.  
I can offer your money back, because there is nothing we can do here and now, as long as we hold this as truth.

2\. You see that it would be beneficial for you, if you were mistaken. Thus, you are **actually** open to seeing something new. Not because I want to preach at you or to convince you, but because you are looking for another way to look at this. So you think to yourself: “Maybe there is another way to look at this. What can I lose by asking?” 

#### **5\. WITNESS SHIFTS. (CALL THEM OUT.)**

Be there, present, when insight occurs.  
That's it.  
Sometimes, you'll just say "Yes". Affirming.  
Sometimes, you'll say a few words that come to mind.  
Sometimes, you'll just be silent, smiling, affirming.

But once they got it, once they had the space and time to let it sink in, let THEM talk about it, and be the only person who truly understands. The only eye witness.

Such that even if they later think "Ehhh, I was probably just 'high' on the nice conversation, there was nothing special," they can remember that they weren't the only person in the room when the world shifted.

That will help them remember what they once saw, and that it is true.  
Even outside of your sessions, they will know that you know.  
This will help their resilience and solidify their peace of mind.

#### **6\. HELP ME PUT IT INTO ACTION**

   
A true insight is priceless and has immeasurable intrinsic value.  
For my insight to have any effect on my world, action is often necessary \- or at least useful.  
Insight put into action is what changes the world.  
Don't rush it. First let it sink in and integrate, give it space. Let me cook.  
Sometimes it will need more time than just a few minutes during that call.

Then, when I am present and enthusiastic, full of inner movement, help me find a small and easy way to get on the path of action.

The first step is always enough to get on track, but as my coach, you can help me much more than that.

Yet, don't do it all at once. Don't overplan from the start. Let me move first. Keep it simple.  
Once I do, your support will help me stay on track, know I'm on the right track, and when to stop or deviate.  
Keep it simple.

Remember: Sometimes, the next step for me is to hang up the phone and stay with what I saw.  
Please help me with this. Lead and bring the call to a close, if that's serves me best.

# **5\. Steps To Learn Coaching**

#### **ART vs. CRAFT**

Coaching is a craft, not an art. You're doing a good craft, like every other good craftsman.Software developers, plumbers, and writers all do their craft. As a coach, you do yours.  
Their tools include wrenches and text editors. Your tools are listening, understanding, and questioning.

Coaching is often turned into an art.  
Art sells better, is harder to replicate and you're cool, suave, and fancy by being an artist. I get it.  
People pride themselves by saying stuff like "I don't have a coaching method", "There is no formula" or "I just pick up my phone and see what comes up in the moment."  
All of that is fine. I said all of that multiple times. I still do.  
Yet, that is not the whole truth.

The person without a method is blind to his own method.  
He thinks he is this unique free-spirited butterfly guided by “god”.  
Which, again, is not the whole truth.  
He repeats the words he heard from others, structures his sessions in the same way he did two weeks ago and has rather fixed criteria for ending a session or the way he leads the conversation.  
Subconsciously, he knows what he is doing.  
Hopefully\! Or he doesn't.  
Please, be the one who knows what he is doing. Focus on the basics to build a bulletproof basement for your coaching. Like a experienced musician. 

#### **The Guitar Parable**

Compare Coaching to playing the guitar, if you want to stay artsy.

I have no idea about playing the guitar.  
But I know that, without having learned how to operate a guitar, you can sit down and channel god's message all day long, and it won't come out as intended.

So before you can be all 'Joe Satriani' or 'Steve Vai' with your guitar, you have to practice. A lot. And that practice will probably not consist of just improvising.  
It will probably include steps like:  
learning to HOLD the guitar,  
learning rhythm,  
learning to stroke/pluck the right strings,  
learning to change the frequency of the sounds by pushing down on different strings with the at first cramped fingers of your other hand.

Then, when you know the basics of this device, you'll replicate a few songs. Start with easy to play songs and progress to harder songs, so practicing will be actually fun.

A lot of time will pass.

# **6\. The Evolution of a Coaching Relationship**

Here, I outline how a Coaching Relationship in a classical setting of “Performance Coaching” evolves. This might help you to get an insight on how your relationships with your clients evolve and what is needed at what stage.

I keep saying "Coaching isn't a haircut" and by now, you might know what I mean.  
It should be truly “to coach **with** sb.”. It is a necessary requirement that the client brings his part of the power, that he co-owns the call and co-creates the conversation.

Coaching comes from sports and athletics. A coach cannot train the athlete. The athlete has to practice and exercise and train. Yet, every elite athlete has at least one coach. Coaching in sports is so obvious we forget that even every tiny kickers team of five year olds has at least one coach.

What does a coach do in sports?

For simplicity’s sake I will couple the stages of a relationship with stages of the progress of development. The attributes your client develops can be skill, performance, fitness, income, level of happiness, level of understanding etc \-whatever your client wants you to help them increase. For sports we will focus on increasing skill and performance in the particular sport. All of what is written applies to all other domains.

**1\.** At the **beginner level**, where the coach has much more experience and skill in what the athlete wants to learn, the most helpful thing is just giving simple directions, i.e. he will tell him what to do.  
This is more consulting or teaching than coaching. It gets the ball rolling.  
The feedback that a coach gives at this point is based on his taste built through his own experience in the field.  
Another valuable thing a coach provides at this level is accountability. If you agree with your coach to do one clean pull-up every day, and you know he will ask you next Wednesday, you're much more likely to do it. In most cases, it is easier for your brain to make you do this than to think up a clean story why you couldn't to explain yourself to the coach.  
The goal here is 'on-boarding' the athlete and getting him up to speed in the sport. Learning the basics, getting the feet wet, practicing the fundamentals, accumulating general experience. With that and the immense amount of feedback from all that attempting and failing, skill is built and the maximum possible performance is increased.

**2\.** At the **advanced level**, a coach’s athletic performance is similar to the athletes, but his technique and/or experience in the field are still above the level of the athlete. Now the main characteristic of what a coach  brings to the table is his taste and his feedback based on that. The main challenge is persistence and continuity of the athlete. The main roadblocks are larger injuries, mental problems, or other "setbacks". I put "setbacks" in quotes, because the obstacle IS the way. Here the previous growth is put to the test. Here the quantity of the progress is boiled down in size but increases in quality concentration. If not before that, here the mental aspects are way more important than the physiological and a coach can be very valuable if he knows how to navigate in times of crisis. Naturally, psychotherapists are usually more specialized on this than the typical personal trainer at a gym.

**3\.** At the **world-class level**, a coach’s athletic performance as well as technique are usually lower than the athletes’. The coach’s main benefit is the unshakable belief in the athlete’s power and ability to win. At this level the accountability the coach was providing basically plays the last role of his value to the athlete. The athlete is usually more committed to himself, his teammates if any, and his goal of winning than to the coach. The same is true for the coach’s own experience in the field. Usually this takes a back seat, as "the game" is different for his client than it was for him, and a good coach knows that. So there will be only infrequent "back then, when I was cycling the tour de france in 1998" stories. However, a new kind of taste is built over time. That taste is not gained by your own experience participating in that field but by being with and coaching THAT athlete (and to a lesser extent, other athletes in the same field). It is the kind of "that's not the swing I know you can do, that's not your swing, Brian" and the "Yes, there you go, and now give me a few more of these. That's what we need\!" kind of feedback. This ties in with the mental stability gained by working with a coach who is unshakable like a rock, who will be with you no matter what and who will always tell you the truth \- and NOW what you want to hear. Like the best kind of friend would. I still can not see how a coach can be a good coach without being a good friend.

Good coaching works. It works so well that other authors can cite hundreds of companies reporting a 5-10x return on investment (ROI). I don't need to convince you since you're a coach already. You know that it can change your client's world in a blink of an eye. That's why we all love this job.

# **7\. List of Powerful Coaching Tools to introduce**

#### **Agreement vs. Expectation (I got this from Steve Chandler)**

People struggle with disappointment in life. All of it is optional. Disappointment is realizing your expectation is in sharp contrast with your reality. Feeling disappointed is a love letter from your emotional system that your thinking "how it should be" is not "how it is". Since it is off, it can be safely disregarded. When you do, you'll feel good again.

I've listened and re-listened to Steve Chandler's 30 minute 10$ audio file on Expectation vs. Agreement countless times.  
I've learned how I can create strong agreements with people instead of expecting them to do something.  
I highly recommend you listen to this twice.

You can create a culture with your clients where both of you know that:  
a. Nothing is to be expected unless it is agreed upon  
and  
b. Everything that is agreed upon is basically a safe bet.

#### **ANY Request \- and the full list of acceptable Answers**

Let them know that you are willing to make an experiment with them. You and them will both agree to be encouraged to ask for ANYTHING.

The catch? The acceptable answers for any request are "YES (for now)" and "NO (for now)" can be followed by "I'd like to re-negotiate".

"I don't really know if this is the right time" can not be a "Yes"  
"I'm not sure" is a "No, for now"  
Both can be "re-negotiated".

It is amazing what happens when people learn to ask for what they want and to say "No" to what they don't.  
Hearing and saying "No" is not quite encouraged in our upbringing.  
Whenever we said "Yes" everyone in our family was happy, whenever we said "No" it usually went all "Why? What's wrong with those onions? Do you want to make your Mommy sad?" And even more manipulative kind of child abuse.  
Same goes for requests. Many of us learned early to consider our asks before asking.

We've experienced entitled, defensive behaviour from innocent people. They were just protecting their self image vehemently, like:  
"WHAT? NOOO\!"  
"What a ridiculous thing to ask for\!"  
"NO, I don't want to go out with you, ugh, what's your problem?"

Now, as adults, we're free to ask for whatever we want and to say "No" to whatever we want. And we don't know it. It was a blind spot for 100% of people I worked with, including myself. It could be a blind spot for you too.

You can **feel** the difference between fresh and new thought and conditioned thought.  
One feels stale and limited, often serious and heavy. This often includes direct logical conditional fuckups including instances of “because ...,” “only if ...,” “since I was little …,” and other games of identity and meaning.   
The other feels light, fresh and new. It comes with a feeling of openness and potential. It often feels fun and “true”. This is often directly related to what is here now.

# **8\. Powerful Coaching Questions**

One and the same question can be the most powerful intervention, or completely useless and uninspiring. Context and intention are king. There are no "powerful" questions that bring the power on their own. You, the coach, have to bring the power. The question is just the empty vessel.

I intentionally kept this chapter brief and general. A few questions are all you need to get started. You'll discover new tools as you go and as you'll gain more and more experience coaching and being coached. You will develop and expand your own set of favorite questions over time.

I personally don’t have a list with questions somewhere. I prefer to just **be honestly and openly curious** and see what question comes out of the moment.

This is the key.  
**List of a few Coaching Questions to get you started:**

"What would be helpful today?"

"What do you need right now?"

"Why?" \- This operator lets you explore the belief-system of the client. Use it 5x in sequence to delve into depth.

"What's behind that?'

If the client made something very "difficult" ask:  
"What if it was easy?"

"What would you say to your child if they were in the same situation 20 years from now?"

If the client says "either X or Y" let them explore more options with:  
"What else you got?"

After saying something that came to your mind, you can ask:  
"What do you hear, when I share that distinction / idea / metaphor...?"

Play. Experiment. See what works and what doesn't. Continue playing.

# **10\. How To Use Your Coach \- getting the most out of being powerfully coached**

This book describes how good coaching works.

**If you know how something works, it's easier to use it.**

Most people didn't learn how to use a coach properly. And it's not their fault. Being coached powerfully is something we learn by doing. And if you're a good coach, you will help your client to apply you, their tool, where they see the greatest leverage.

When I had my first real coach, I always prepared a list of questions before each session. And for the first few sessions I was trying to get through the long list and didn't feel satisfied until every question was answered to appease my intellect. 

That was rough.

As a coach, you understand that giving your clients the answer is like giving your clients a fish. This will not transform them. They'll be in need of “**your** fish”.

Instead of giving answers, get people on the path to find their own answers \- their own insights. They will deeply understand and “just know” for life. Over time working with their coach, they will have learned where to find Insights \- learned “**how to** fish”.   
This skill that you've built together, they will keep it for life \- long after they’ve stopped paying you.

Being coached is far from passive. Coaching is not a haircut.

''I can only show you the door.''   
\- *Morpheus, The Matrix*

Your clients have to get on the path and find their own answers.  
THEY have to do this.  
You can only show them the door.

I like to get my clients on board. I might say something like:  
“If you want to get the most out of our time together, you can co-own and co-create it. If you know, you can suggest how to best lead you and where. That doesn't mean I will do all of what you propose. But it gives me valuable information about what you think is important and where you think the gold is hidden.”

As a coach, you probably like hearing sentences like:

''Is there something I need to hear that I am not hearing?''

''I'd love to go deeper here, I have the feeling there's something important beneath the surface. Can you help me?''

''What I fear is \_\_\_\_. Maybe this is unrelated. I don't know if this is something we should talk about. What do you think?''

''Whenever you start talking with me about \_\_\_\_, I get defensive and block. I notice this, but don't know what to do. Will you help me solve this?''

''What do you think, are we currently using the full depth of our conversation together? Can you help me go deeper and see something new on this topic?''

''I wanted to share something with you. I'm a little vulnerable around that. Would you like to help me be cool with sharing it?''

Yes, these are the sentences we love to hear as coaches.  
They are unfakeable steps of the coachee towards more depth.  
They will naturally bring the best coach out of you.

Pro tip: As a coach, you can use the above examples to create powerful coaching questions to help your client go there. After all, you're paid to lead.

Take the lead and go where you need to go to do your job.

''Where wouldn't I have gone?''

''What's the deeper place we haven't yet looked at?''

''What's the thing you don't want to say?''

''What's behind that?''

''What's REALLY behind that?''

''What would we have missed if we stopped here?''

PLAY.   
Whether you're coaching or being coached.   
PLAY.   
You won’t get to new places by taking everything serious and attempting not to make any mistakes. 

When you learn juggling, just embrace that balls will fall on the ground. It’s much easier to pick ‘em up than to try to never let one fall.

Pitfalls: 

1. Making your coach 100% responsible for your coaching. Thinking “I paid you. Now show me what you got\!”  
   Coaching is still not a haircut.  
2. No prep time.  
   Arriving on time to your coaching session with tons of daily noise on your mind, not being in the right state of mind. Yes, a good coach will help you get there in the first part of the session, but whenever possible save yourself and your coach the trouble by just arriving 15 minutes early to your session and settling in. Becoming present with what’s up and being curious whether you might have an intention for today.  
3. No integration time.  
   It is even more important to block time for integration after. An insight will have no time to shine if you have scheduled a call with your accountant, back-to-back after your session.  
   You alone have the opportunity to let the insight land properly, integrate fully and embody the new reality you now inhabit. That is when a lot of the transformation takes place.  
   Create space in advance to take a walk alone, have a relaxing bath with instrumental music. Most people continue to see new things. Some clients tell me this is the time with the highest density of insight for them. Here, time with yourself will multiply the value you're getting out of the session. I guarantee it.

# **11\. Why You’re Broke...**

Before I get started, I want to make one thing sure. I’m not a multi-millionaire and I wouldn’t consider myself as a money guru. I can speak from an experience of financing my live standards and meanwhile feeding my family while working as a coach. The following tips helped me on this way. I hope, they’ll inspire you too to build up your own financially fulfilling life.  

You’re broke because you don't **specialize**.  
... because you think you can solve *almost* every problem for *nearly everyone*. Just because you theoretically can, doesn't make it a good idea for me to buy it from you.

Generalist in action:  
A: "What do you do?"  
B: "I'm a coach."  
A: "What kind of coach?"  
B: "I serve a variety of clients. I do life-coaching, but I also help people with their business and their performance in sports\! You know, this coaching thing is powerful. I can solve every problem."

Even if this is true, the other person doesn't know specifically how you can be beneficial to them.

An expert in action:  
A: "What do you do?"  
B: "I'm helping coaches to build their businesses by powerfully coaching them and helping them to find their authentic marketing voice \- you know? How to ‘actually’ sell from the heart, not out of fear."  
A: "That's interesting. I'm a dentist, not a coach, but I could use some of that."  
B: "I've never worked with a dentist before, but I'd love to find out if there is a fit. If you would like to explore, let's talk."

This is not a technique. I'm not telling you to say you're coaching coaches to attract dentists. If you and the dentist aren't a fit, say "No" and help them find the right person.

Always remember to talk about your clients life, not coaching as a concept. Nobody cares about coaching. Everyone wants a better, more fulfilled life.

You’re broke because you say "Yes" to non- profitable agreements.

You might think: "a \[little money\] is better than no money.”

The actual statement you're making is  
"\[Not enough money to sustain a healthy and strong business\] is better than no money.”

Both statements are wrong if you want to sustain a healthy business. I’m sure you see the point.

Thus, only agree to profitable deals. Make this a **business policy**. If I was your boss and you would accept non-profitable deals, we would have to talk. If you continued, I'd fire you. \- or go bankrupt.

Chances are: you’re your own boss.  
Make sure to **only accept profitable deals** from now on. You might have to do your homework and determine what that means for you specifically. Please do that. 

Know your minimum level of engagement. Know what you’re offering and how much it must at least cost so your business thrives. 

Example:  
Let’s say you established, your business needs a minimum of 2000€ per engagement to grow and be strong. This would mean that you understood that “generating new prospects” and having free intake conversations, keeping the lights on, paying the bills, etc. makes it necessary that the minimum payment of a new client has to be above 2000€ to not run losses, financially.

I’m a fan of doing pro-bono or charitable work, when you feel called to do so. When you do, resolve to not make a profit and help for free. This then is not business but more like a hobby. Understand this, and when you want more monetary growth, focus on the profitable agreements.

# **12\. Epilogue \- Your Journey in a Nutshell**

Be coached powerfully. Have your life change, forever.  
Then live that new life with a full heart.  
Cultivate your desire to love and to share that feeling with the people around you.

Listen to what they tell you about their lives. Really care about what they are sharing with you. Understand.  
Then, if you can help somehow, ask if they would like your help. Maybe a recommendation for a movie / book / course / therapist / dentist / lawyer or if what they are describing is what you can personally help with, ask them if they want your help.

Look, this is not a coaching thing. If you are a plumber, this will be the same process. What I describe will build relationships and community. This will help people.

Know what you can and can't do. Communicate that openly.  
Let me share a story from my own experience. When I first started coaching, I struggled with finding clients and establishing my brand. I felt overwhelmed and unsure of how to navigate the competitive landscape. However, I realized that I needed to focus on my strengths and unique value proposition. By doing so, I was able to create a niche for myself and attract clients who resonated with my approach. This experience taught me the importance of self-awareness and authenticity in building a successful coaching business.  
Be professional about your service. Never work without a clear assignment or permission. Being professional comes before the money, not after.

You can have free intake conversations.   
Have them be powerful and life-changing.   
Show, don't tell.

Then, if they want more, they will ask you how they can continue working with you. When you know what you're doing, more and more people will be lining up for you to help them. Put a price on working with you and say that number out loud.   
Say it to the people you would like to work with. Collect the 'NO's. Don’t continue working for free with the NO-people out of insecurity.  
Consider the story of Coach Sarah, who initially offered her coaching services for free to gain experience. She found herself overwhelmed with clients who didn't value her time and expertise. Realizing this, she decided to set clear boundaries and started charging for her services. This shift not only improved her client relationships but also increased her confidence and professionalism.  
First, don't stress, and don't make that "to work with me is a one year in advance investment of €20k, which is non-refundable."

I did that. I can’t recommend it.   
You will have time for that later. In the meantime, fill your practice. Choose a size, maybe 5 or 10 or 15 clients, and fill your practice with regular clients who pay you money.   
Want to sell a subscription which can always be cancelled? Do it.   
Want to sell two-months-packages for a fixed total? Do it.   
Want to sell a package of six or twelve sessions? Bi-weekly? Or used on demand? Do it.  
Want to offer individual one-off sessions at a slightly higher price? Do it. 

Play with it. 

As long as you don't have a full practice of paying clients, make it easy for people to work with you.   
This is the top priority. Fill your practice first.

Create safe boundaries with your clients using agreements. 

Be truly fearless. Say what nobody else will say. That doesn't mean being mean and verbally abusive to your clients for the sake of being impactful.   
Power vs. Force, my friend.   
But always have the real talk when you see it. Some real talk is correcting negative self perception. Some real talk is correcting delusion, confusion or even hybris. All is good. Don’t hold back one of these out of insecurity.

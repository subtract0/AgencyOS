# Landing Page Evaluation Rubric

## Purpose
This rubric provides objective criteria for evaluating landing page quality. Used by both AI judges (DeepSeek-R1 + o1-mini) and human reviewers to score landing pages on a 0-10 scale across 5 critical dimensions.

## Target Threshold
- **Hero Section**: ≥8.0/10 overall (all dimensions ≥7.0)
- **Full Landing Page**: ≥8.0/10 overall (all dimensions ≥7.0)

---

## Dimension 1: Clarity

**Definition**: Is the value proposition crystal clear? Can a reader understand what this product does and who it's for in <5 seconds?

### Scoring Guidelines

**10/10 - Exceptional Clarity**
- Value proposition stated in <10 words
- Benefit is immediately obvious
- No jargon, no ambiguity
- Example: "Track your energy patterns in 5 minutes a day"

**8/10 - Strong Clarity**
- Value proposition clear but slightly wordy (10-15 words)
- Benefit clear with minimal interpretation needed
- Minimal jargon, well-explained if present
- Example: "Discover what drains your energy and what restores it with gentle daily tracking"

**6/10 - Acceptable Clarity**
- Value proposition requires 2-3 seconds to parse
- Benefit stated but not immediately obvious
- Some jargon that may confuse readers
- Example: "Leverage AI-powered insights to optimize your spiritual wellness journey"

**4/10 - Weak Clarity**
- Value proposition vague or buried
- Benefit requires interpretation
- Heavy jargon without explanation
- Example: "Transform your consciousness with next-generation biometric feedback loops"

**2/10 - Very Weak Clarity**
- No clear value proposition
- Benefit unclear even after reading multiple times
- Incomprehensible jargon
- Example: "Synergize quantum mindfulness paradigms for holistic actualization"

### Red Flags (Automatic -2 points)
- "AI-powered" without explaining what AI actually does
- Generic promises ("transform your life", "unlock potential")
- No concrete outcome mentioned

---

## Dimension 2: Specificity

**Definition**: Concrete details vs. vague promises. Does the copy use specific numbers, timeframes, features, or outcomes?

### Scoring Guidelines

**10/10 - Exceptionally Specific**
- Specific timeframes ("5 minutes a day", "14-day trial")
- Concrete numbers ("Join 10,000+ seekers", "Track 3 key energy patterns")
- Named features (not generic)
- Measurable outcomes ("85% feel more centered after 1 week")
- Example: "Track your energy in 5 minutes daily. Identify 3 core patterns. Join 10,000+ spiritual seekers."

**8/10 - Strongly Specific**
- Multiple specific details (timeframe + number or feature + outcome)
- Concrete examples given
- Few generic statements
- Example: "Daily 5-minute check-ins reveal your unique energy patterns and suggest personalized practices"

**6/10 - Acceptably Specific**
- Some specific details (timeframe OR number OR feature)
- Mix of concrete and vague
- Example: "Quick daily practices help you understand your spiritual rhythms"

**4/10 - Weakly Specific**
- Mostly vague, with 1-2 specific details
- Generic benefits dominate
- Example: "Regular practice brings clarity to your spiritual life"

**2/10 - Very Vague**
- No specific details
- All generic promises
- Example: "Enhance your spiritual journey with our comprehensive platform"

### Green Flags (Add specificity points)
- Exact timeframe mentioned: +1
- Specific social proof number: +1
- Measurable outcome: +1
- Named feature (not "our tool", but "Energy Pattern Tracker"): +1

---

## Dimension 3: Emotional Resonance

**Definition**: Does the copy hit pain points hard? Does it use direct quotes from user research? Does it make the reader feel seen?

### Scoring Guidelines

**10/10 - Exceptionally Resonant**
- Uses direct quotes from pain point research
- Mirrors exact user language
- Reader sees themselves in the copy
- Pain points are visceral, not abstract
- Example: "I have no idea what drains my energy" → "Discover what drains your energy and what restores it"

**8/10 - Strongly Resonant**
- Paraphrases user pain points accurately
- Addresses emotional needs, not just functional
- Creates "that's exactly me" moment
- Example: "Feeling disconnected from your spiritual practice because you can't find 30 minutes? We built this for you."

**6/10 - Acceptably Resonant**
- Mentions common pain points
- Some emotional language
- Generic enough to apply broadly
- Example: "Busy schedule making it hard to stay spiritually connected? We can help."

**4/10 - Weakly Resonant**
- Generic pain points not specific to target audience
- Minimal emotional language
- Doesn't create identification
- Example: "Want to improve your spiritual life? Try our app."

**2/10 - Not Resonant**
- No pain points mentioned
- Product-focused, not user-focused
- No emotional connection
- Example: "Our app has many features for spiritual development"

### Required Elements for ≥8/10
- At least 1 direct quote from user research
- Pain point stated before solution
- Emotional state described ("feeling disconnected", "overwhelmed", "lost")

---

## Dimension 4: Believability

**Definition**: Does this sound real or like marketing fluff? Are claims backed by evidence? Would a skeptical reader trust this?

### Scoring Guidelines

**10/10 - Exceptionally Believable**
- Specific, verifiable claims
- Social proof with context ("10,000+ users in 6 months")
- No superlatives without proof
- Realistic outcomes stated
- Acknowledges limitations or effort required
- Example: "85% of users report feeling more centered after 1 week of daily 5-minute check-ins. Results vary based on consistency."

**8/10 - Strongly Believable**
- Claims backed by numbers or testimonials
- Realistic timeframes
- Few superlatives, all supported
- Example: "Join 10,000+ spiritual seekers who've built consistent practices with 5-minute daily sessions"

**6/10 - Acceptably Believable**
- Some social proof
- Mix of realistic and optimistic claims
- Occasional unsupported superlative
- Example: "Thousands of users have transformed their spiritual practice. Start your journey today!"

**4/10 - Weakly Believable**
- Vague social proof ("many users")
- Unrealistic claims ("transform in days")
- Multiple unsupported superlatives
- Example: "The revolutionary platform that's changing lives worldwide. Experience miraculous shifts in your consciousness."

**2/10 - Not Believable**
- No proof
- Extreme claims
- Feels like hype
- Example: "This will completely transform your life and solve all your spiritual struggles forever"

### Red Flags (Automatic -2 points each)
- "Revolutionary" without explaining why
- "#1" or "Best" without citation
- "Guaranteed" transformation
- "Miracle", "breakthrough", "game-changer" without context

---

## Dimension 5: Pain-Alignment

**Definition**: Does the solution directly address the extracted pain points? Is there a clear connection between research and copy?

### Scoring Guidelines

**10/10 - Perfect Alignment**
- Every bullet/feature maps to a specific extracted pain point
- Uses exact pain point language from research
- Solution is obviously designed for these pain points
- Example: Pain point "Lost my spiritual routine after moving" → Feature "Rebuild consistency with 5-min practices that work anywhere"

**8/10 - Strong Alignment**
- Most features address extracted pain points
- Paraphrases pain point language accurately
- Clear connection between problem and solution
- Example: Pain point about time scarcity → "5 minutes a day" repeatedly emphasized

**6/10 - Acceptable Alignment**
- Some features address pain points
- Generic solutions that could address pain points
- Connection present but not explicit
- Example: Pain point about tracking → Mentions tracking feature but doesn't explain how it solves the problem

**4/10 - Weak Alignment**
- Few features map to extracted pain points
- Solutions seem disconnected from research
- Generic features not tailored to audience
- Example: Pain points about energy and patterns → Features focus on community and content instead

**2/10 - No Alignment**
- Features don't address any extracted pain points
- Copy ignores research
- Solution seems designed for different audience
- Example: Pain points about spiritual practice → Copy focuses on productivity and performance

### Required for ≥8/10
- Minimum 3 features/bullets directly address extracted pain points
- At least 1 direct quote or paraphrase from pain point research
- Clear "Before" (pain) → "After" (solution) story

---

## Composite Scoring

### Overall Score Calculation
```
Overall Score = (Clarity + Specificity + Emotional_Resonance + Believability + Pain_Alignment) / 5
```

### Minimum Thresholds
- **Hero Section**: All dimensions ≥7.0, Overall ≥8.0
- **Full Page**: All dimensions ≥7.0, Overall ≥8.0

### Pass/Fail Criteria
- **PASS**: Overall ≥8.0 AND no dimension <7.0
- **REFINEMENT NEEDED**: Overall 6.0-7.9 OR any dimension <7.0
- **FAIL**: Overall <6.0 OR any dimension <5.0

## Example Scores

| Dimension | Score | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Clarity | 4/10 | Vague value prop, "transform your life" is generic |
| Specificity | 2/10 | No timeframes, numbers, or concrete details |
| Emotional Resonance | 3/10 | No pain points mentioned, product-focused |
| Believability | 5/10 | "AI" without explanation, "transform" overpromises |
| Pain-Alignment | 7/10 | Assumes AI solves problems but doesn't explain how |
| **OVERALL** | **4.2/10** | **FAIL** - Too vague, not aligned with research |

| Dimension | Score | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Clarity | 9/10 | Value prop in <10 words, benefit immediately obvious |
| Specificity | 8.5/10 | "5 minutes a day" specific, concrete outcome "reclaim practice" |
| Emotional Resonance | 9/10 | Addresses time scarcity pain point, "reclaim" implies loss |
| Believability | 8/10 | Realistic timeframe, modest claim, no hype |
| Pain-Alignment | 9.5/10 | Directly quotes research "can't find 30 minutes", offers 5-min alternative |
| **OVERALL** | **8.8/10** | **PASS** - Clear, specific, emotionally resonant, believable, aligned |

## Usage Guidelines

### For AI Judges (DeepSeek-R1, o1-mini)
1. Score each dimension independently (0-10)
2. Provide specific quotes/examples supporting each score
3. Identify which rubric level (10/8/6/4/2) best matches
4. List improvement suggestions per dimension
5. Calculate overall score
6. Flag any red flags that trigger automatic deductions

### For Human Reviewers
1. Read landing page completely
2. Score each dimension based on gut feel, then calibrate against rubric
3. Check if scores align with AI judges (expect ±1 point variance)
4. Override AI scores if strongly disagree (document why)
5. Final decision on pass/fail/refinement

### For Ensemble Judging
1. DeepSeek-R1 scores independently
2. o1-mini scores independently
3. Average scores per dimension
4. Human reviewer sees both AI scores + average
5. Human makes final call (can override average)
